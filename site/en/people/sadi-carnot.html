Sadi Carnot

  • Lifespan: 1796 – 1832
  • Fields: Physics, Engineering
  • Countries: France

Overview

Nicolas Leonard Sadi Carnot was a French military engineer and physicist, often described as the father of thermodynamics. His only published work introduced the concept of the Carnot cycle and established the theoretical limits of heat engine efficiency.

Early Life

Carnot was born in 1796 in Paris, the son of the prominent military leader and mathematician Lazare Carnot. He was educated at the Ecole Polytechnique and the Ecole du Genie, both elite French military schools, before pursuing his interest in the theory of heat engines.

Impact

Carnot's analysis of heat engines established the second law of thermodynamics in embryonic form and defined the maximum possible efficiency of any heat engine. His work, rediscovered by Clapeyron and later Clausius and Kelvin, became the foundation of thermodynamic theory.
