Max Planck

  • Lifespan: 1858 – 1947
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: Germany

Overview

Max Planck was a German physicist who originated quantum theory by introducing the concept of energy quanta to explain blackbody radiation. His revolutionary insight that energy is emitted in discrete packets marked the birth of modern physics.

Early Life

Born in Kiel, Germany, Planck came from an academic family. He studied at the Universities of Munich and Berlin under Kirchhoff and Helmholtz, initially focusing on thermodynamics before turning his attention to the problem of blackbody radiation.

Impact

Planck's quantum hypothesis fundamentally changed physics by revealing that energy at the atomic scale comes in discrete units. His constant, h, is one of the most fundamental constants in nature, and his work opened the door to quantum mechanics, one of the pillars of modern physics.

Timeline

  • 1879 — Earned his PhD from the University of Munich with a thesis on the second law of thermodynamics.
  • 1889 — Became professor at the University of Berlin.
  • 1900 — Introduced the quantum hypothesis to solve the blackbody radiation problem.
  • 1918 — Awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics for his discovery of energy quanta.
  • 1930 — Became president of the Kaiser Wilhelm Society (later Max Planck Society).

Famous Works

  • On the Law of Distribution of Energy in the Normal Spectrum (1900) — The paper introducing the quantum hypothesis and Planck's constant to explain blackbody radiation.
  • The Theory of Heat Radiation (1906) — Comprehensive treatise on radiation theory incorporating quantum concepts.
  • Introduction to Theoretical Physics (1916) — Multi-volume textbook series covering the major branches of theoretical physics.
