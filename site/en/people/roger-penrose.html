Roger Penrose

  • Lifespan: 1931 – present
  • Fields: Physics, Mathematics
  • Countries: United Kingdom

Overview

Roger Penrose is a British mathematical physicist who made fundamental contributions to general relativity, including the singularity theorems proving that black hole formation is an inevitable consequence of gravitational collapse. He was awarded the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics for this work.

Early Life

Penrose was born in Colchester, England into an intellectually distinguished family. He studied mathematics at University College London and earned his Ph.D. from Cambridge, later holding professorships at Birkbeck College and the University of Oxford.

Impact

Penrose's singularity theorems fundamentally changed the understanding of general relativity and black holes. His work on Penrose tilings, twistor theory, and the connections between mathematics, physics, and consciousness has inspired research across multiple disciplines.
