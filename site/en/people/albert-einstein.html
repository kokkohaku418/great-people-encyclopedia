Albert Einstein

  • Lifespan: 1879 – 1955
  • Fields: Physics, Mathematics
  • Countries: Germany, Switzerland, United States

Overview

Albert Einstein was a German-born theoretical physicist who developed the theory of relativity, one of the two pillars of modern physics. His work on the photoelectric effect earned him the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential scientists of all time.

Early Life

Einstein was born in Ulm, in the Kingdom of Württemberg in the German Empire, on 14 March 1879. His family moved to Munich shortly after his birth. He showed an early aptitude for mathematics and physics, teaching himself algebra and Euclidean geometry at age twelve. He attended the Swiss Federal Polytechnic in Zürich, graduating in 1900.

Impact

Einstein's theories of relativity revolutionized our understanding of space, time, and gravity. His mass-energy equivalence formula E=mc² became the most famous equation in physics. His work laid the foundation for quantum mechanics, cosmology, and nuclear energy. He fundamentally changed how humanity understands the universe.

Famous Works

  • Special Theory of Relativity (1905) — Introduced the concept that the laws of physics are the same for all non-accelerating observers and that the speed of light is constant regardless of the observer's motion.
  • General Theory of Relativity (1915) — Extended special relativity to include gravity, describing it as a curvature of spacetime caused by mass and energy.
  • Photoelectric Effect (1905) — Explained the photoelectric effect by proposing that light consists of discrete quanta of energy, laying groundwork for quantum theory.
  • Mass-Energy Equivalence (E=mc²) (1905) — Demonstrated that mass and energy are interchangeable, expressed in the famous equation E=mc².
  • Brownian Motion (1905) — Provided empirical evidence for the existence of atoms by explaining the random motion of particles suspended in fluid.
