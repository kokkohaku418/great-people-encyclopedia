Kip Thorne

  • Lifespan: 1940 – present
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: United States

Overview

Kip Thorne is an American theoretical physicist who made pioneering contributions to gravitational physics and astrophysics. He shared the 2017 Nobel Prize in Physics for the first direct detection of gravitational waves by the LIGO experiment, which he co-founded.

Early Life

Thorne was born in Logan, Utah and showed early aptitude for science. He earned his Ph.D. at Princeton University under John Archibald Wheeler and became one of the youngest full professors in Caltech's history at age 30.

Impact

Thorne's co-founding of the LIGO project led to one of the most significant experimental achievements in physics: the detection of gravitational waves from merging black holes. His theoretical work on wormholes and time warps also contributed to the scientific accuracy of the film Interstellar.

Famous Works

  • Black Holes and Time Warps: Einstein's Outrageous Legacy (1994) — Popular science book exploring the physics of black holes, wormholes, and the nature of spacetime.
  • Gravitation (1973) — Landmark graduate textbook on general relativity co-authored with Charles Misner and John Wheeler, widely known as 'MTW'.
  • The Science of Interstellar (2014) — Book explaining the real science behind the film Interstellar, covering black holes, wormholes, and higher dimensions.
