Voltaire

  • Lifespan: 1694 – 1778
  • Fields: Philosophy, Literature
  • Countries: France

Overview

Voltaire was a French Enlightenment writer, philosopher, and satirist known for his wit, his advocacy of civil liberties, and his criticism of the Catholic Church.

Early Life

Voltaire was born François-Marie Arouet in Paris. He was educated by Jesuits and became famous for his sharp social commentary, which led to his imprisonment in the Bastille.

Impact

Voltaire's advocacy of freedom of speech, separation of church and state, and religious tolerance profoundly influenced the French and American revolutions and modern democratic thought.
