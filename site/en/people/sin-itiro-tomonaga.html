Sin-Itiro Tomonaga

  • Lifespan: 1906 – 1979
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: Japan

Overview

Sin-Itiro Tomonaga was a Japanese theoretical physicist who shared the 1965 Nobel Prize in Physics for his independent development of quantum electrodynamics. Working largely in isolation in wartime Japan, he developed a relativistically covariant formulation of QED.

Early Life

Tomonaga was born in Tokyo and grew up in Kyoto, where his father was a professor of philosophy. He studied at Kyoto Imperial University alongside Hideki Yukawa and later worked with Werner Heisenberg in Leipzig.

Impact

Tomonaga's renormalization technique in quantum electrodynamics, developed independently of Schwinger and Feynman, was crucial for making QED a consistent and predictive theory. He also mentored a generation of Japanese theoretical physicists.

  • Super-Many-Time Theory (1943) — A relativistically covariant formulation of quantum field theory using multiple time variables for each particle.
  • Renormalization of QED (1948) — Independently developed the renormalization procedure to remove infinities from quantum electrodynamics calculations.
