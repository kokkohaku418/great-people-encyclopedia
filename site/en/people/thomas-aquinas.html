Thomas Aquinas

  • Lifespan: 1225 – 1274
  • Fields: Philosophy
  • Countries: Italy

Overview

Thomas Aquinas was an Italian Dominican friar and philosopher-theologian who synthesized Aristotelian philosophy with Christian theology. He is the most influential philosopher in the Catholic tradition.

Early Life

Aquinas was born in Roccasecca, Italy, to a noble family. Against his family's wishes, he joined the Dominican order and studied under Albertus Magnus in Cologne and Paris.

Impact

Aquinas's Summa Theologica became the definitive work of Catholic philosophy and theology. His synthesis of faith and reason, and his natural law theory, remain influential in philosophy, law, and ethics.
