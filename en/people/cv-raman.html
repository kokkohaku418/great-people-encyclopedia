C.V. Raman

  • Lifespan: 1888 – 1970
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: India

Overview

Chandrasekhara Venkata Raman was an Indian physicist who discovered the Raman effect, in which light changes wavelength when scattered by molecules. This discovery earned him the 1930 Nobel Prize in Physics, making him the first Asian to receive a Nobel Prize in science.

Early Life

Raman was born in Tiruchirappalli, British India, to a family of scholars. He excelled academically, completing his bachelor's degree at age 16, and initially worked in the Indian Finance Department before pursuing physics full-time.

Impact

The Raman effect became a powerful tool for identifying molecular structures and is now widely used in chemistry, materials science, and medicine through Raman spectroscopy. His Nobel Prize inspired generations of Indian scientists. He also founded the Indian Journal of Physics and the Indian Academy of Sciences.

Timeline
