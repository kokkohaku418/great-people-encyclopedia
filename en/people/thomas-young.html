Thomas Young

  • Lifespan: 1773 – 1829
  • Fields: Physics, Biology
  • Countries: United Kingdom

Overview

Thomas Young was an English polymath who made important contributions to optics, physiology, and Egyptology. He is best known for his double-slit experiment, which demonstrated the wave nature of light and established the principle of interference.

Early Life

Young was born in 1773 in Milverton, Somerset, England, a child prodigy who could read fluently by age two. He studied medicine at London, Edinburgh, and Gottingen, and became a physician, but his scientific curiosity ranged across an astonishing number of disciplines.

Impact

Young's double-slit experiment became one of the most important experiments in physics, providing definitive evidence for the wave theory of light. His work on color vision and elasticity (Young's modulus) also had lasting influence on physics and engineering.
