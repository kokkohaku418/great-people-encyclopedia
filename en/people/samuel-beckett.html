Samuel Beckett

  • Lifespan: 1906 – 1989
  • Fields: Literature
  • Countries: ie, France

Overview

Samuel Beckett was an Irish novelist and playwright who was one of the last modernists and a key figure in the Theatre of the Absurd. He won the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1969.

Early Life

Beckett was born in Dublin and studied at Trinity College Dublin. He moved to Paris in 1937, where he became part of James Joyce's circle and spent the rest of his life.

Impact

Beckett's minimalist works stripped theater and prose to their essentials, exploring the human condition with dark humor. Waiting for Godot is one of the most important plays of the 20th century.
