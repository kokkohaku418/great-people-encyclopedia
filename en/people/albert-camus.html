Albert Camus

  • Lifespan: 1913 – 1960
  • Fields: Philosophy, Literature
  • Countries: dz, France

Overview

Albert Camus was a French-Algerian philosopher and author who explored absurdism, the conflict between human desire for meaning and the universe's indifference.

Early Life

Camus was born in Mondovi, French Algeria, to a poor family. He studied philosophy at the University of Algiers and became a journalist and writer.

Impact

Camus' philosophy of the absurd and his literary works profoundly influenced existentialism and postwar thought. He received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1957.
