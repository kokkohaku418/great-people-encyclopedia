Erwin Schrödinger

  • Lifespan: 1887 – 1961
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: Austria

Overview

Erwin Schrödinger was an Austrian physicist who developed wave mechanics, a fundamental formulation of quantum mechanics. He is best known for the Schrödinger equation and his famous thought experiment, Schrödinger's cat. He shared the 1933 Nobel Prize in Physics with Paul Dirac.

Early Life

Schrödinger was born in Vienna, Austria, and was educated at the University of Vienna. He showed early talent in mathematics and physics, earning his doctorate in 1910 and serving in World War I before pursuing an academic career.

Impact

The Schrödinger equation became one of the most important equations in physics, governing the behavior of quantum systems. His wave mechanics formulation provided an alternative to matrix mechanics and is essential to modern chemistry and physics. Schrödinger's cat remains one of the most famous thought experiments in science.
