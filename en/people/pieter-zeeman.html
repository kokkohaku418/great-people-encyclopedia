Pieter Zeeman

  • Lifespan: 1865 – 1943
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: Netherlands

Overview

Pieter Zeeman was a Dutch physicist who discovered the splitting of spectral lines in a magnetic field, known as the Zeeman effect. This discovery provided crucial evidence for the electron theory of matter. He shared the 1902 Nobel Prize in Physics with Hendrik Lorentz.

Early Life

Zeeman was born in Zonnemaire, Netherlands, a small village in Zeeland. He studied physics at the University of Leiden under Hendrik Lorentz and Heike Kamerlingh Onnes, completing his doctorate in 1893.

Impact

The Zeeman effect was instrumental in confirming the existence of electron spin and understanding atomic structure. It became a powerful tool in spectroscopy and astrophysics for measuring magnetic fields. His work supported the classical electron theory of Lorentz and paved the way for quantum mechanics.
