Henri Becquerel

  • Lifespan: 1852 – 1908
  • Fields: Physics
  • Countries: France

Overview

Henri Becquerel was a French physicist who discovered radioactivity in 1896, a finding that fundamentally changed our understanding of matter and energy. He shared the 1903 Nobel Prize in Physics with Pierre and Marie Curie for this groundbreaking work.

Early Life

Born in Paris into a distinguished family of scientists, Becquerel was the son and grandson of physicists. He studied at the École Polytechnique and the École des Ponts et Chaussées, eventually succeeding his father as professor at the Muséum National d'Histoire Naturelle.

Impact

Becquerel's discovery of spontaneous radioactivity opened an entirely new branch of physics and chemistry. His work directly inspired the Curies' research and ultimately led to nuclear physics, nuclear energy, and modern medical treatments using radiation.

Timeline
